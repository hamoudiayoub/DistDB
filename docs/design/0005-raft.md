# 0005 — Raft and the simulator

> Draft written while building Phase 4. Read the Raft paper (Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014) and
> chapters 3, 4 and 6 of Ongaro's thesis, then rewrite this in your own words. Every rule below
> exists because of a specific failure; be able to name it.

## Problem

Keep several copies of a log identical across machines that crash, restart, lose messages and get
partitioned, so that a command, once committed, is never lost or reordered. Later phases replicate
each range of data with it (Phase 5) and run the placement driver on it (Phase 6).

## The shape: a pure state machine

`RaftNode` (`distdb-raft`) has no threads, clocks, disks or sockets. The host feeds it inputs and
carries out its outputs:

```
inputs                         RaftNode                       outputs (Ready)
tick()          ─┐                                       ┌─ hard state, entries, snapshot → persist
step(message)   ─┼─▶  pure, deterministic logic   ──────▶├─ messages                      → send
propose(cmd)    ─┤    (random only via RandomSource)     ├─ committed entries, snapshot   → apply
readIndex(ctx)  ─┘                                       └─ read states                   → answer reads
```

The host loop, for every `Ready`, in this order: **persist** (one synced batch), **send**,
**apply**, then `advance`. Why persist before send: a vote or an acknowledgement is a promise. If a
node votes, crashes before the vote is on disk, and restarts, it can vote again in the same term,
and two leaders get elected. Mutation 5 below shows this happening.

Time is ticks (10 ms in the simulator): elections after 10 to 20 ticks, heartbeats every 2.
Randomized timeouts make split votes rare.

| Piece | Where |
|---|---|
| `RaftNode`, `RaftLog`, `Progress` | `distdb-raft`: the algorithm |
| `Message`, `Entry`, `HardState`, `Snapshot`, `Membership`, `ConfigChange`, `Ready` | `distdb-raft`: data |
| `RaftCodec` | `distdb-raft`: binary encoding for messages and storage |
| `RaftStore` | `distdb-kv`: hard state, log and snapshot in the LSM engine (`0x01 'H'/'L'/'P' group …`) |
| `SimNetwork`, `Life`, `RaftSimulation`, `RaftChecker`, `HashChain` | `distdb-sim`: the simulator |

## The rules, and the failure each prevents

**Elections.**
- A node votes at most once per term, and the vote is on disk before the reply is sent.
  Otherwise there could be two leaders in one term.
- **Election restriction:** a node votes only for a candidate whose log is at least as up to date
  as its own (higher last term, or same last term and at least as long). Otherwise a node missing
  committed entries could be elected and overwrite them. Mutation 1 shows this.
- A new leader appends a **no-op in its term** right away. Without it, entries from earlier terms
  would stay uncommitted until a client wrote something (the next rule), and ReadIndex couldn't
  know the true commit index.

**Replication.**
- **The consistency check:** AppendEntries carries `(prevIndex, prevTerm)`, and a follower accepts
  only if its log has that entry. By induction, two logs that agree on one entry agree on
  everything before it (log matching).
- **Fast backtracking:** a rejection carries the follower's term at `prevIndex` and where that
  term starts. The leader skips a whole term per round trip: 55 conflicting entries take at most
  four messages (`conflictingEntriesAreRepairedATermAtATime`).
- A follower **truncates only uncommitted entries**. `RaftLog` throws if asked to overwrite a
  committed one: that can only be a bug, or a disk that lost synced data.
- A follower **commits only up to the last entry it just verified**, `min(leaderCommit, lastNew)`,
  not its own last index. Entries beyond `lastNew` may be stale leftovers from an old leader.
- Heartbeats are separate messages with no log check. Their commit index is capped at what the
  follower is known to hold.

**The commit rule (Figure 8).** A leader commits by counting replicas **only entries from its own
term**. An entry from an earlier term can sit on a majority and still be overwritten by a later
leader whose log has a higher last term. Once an entry of the current term is on a majority, no
leader lacking it can be elected, so that entry and everything before it are safe.

**The leader counts itself only for entries on its own disk** (`log.stable()`, updated in
`advance`). It must not count entries it has merely appended.

**Leader-side flow control** (`Progress`):
- **PROBE:** one message at a time while finding the match point.
- **REPLICATE:** streams entries optimistically, with up to `maxInflightAppends` unacknowledged.
- **SNAPSHOT:** waits for a snapshot to be acknowledged.

An empty AppendEntries is sent only to probe, or to carry a new commit index, never in answer to
an acknowledgement. Otherwise leader and follower ping-pong forever (see the journal).

**Pre-vote and check-quorum (thesis 9.6).**
- **Pre-vote:** before raising its term, a candidate asks "would you vote for me?". A node cut off
  from the group fails that question forever without pushing everyone's term up, so it can't
  depose a healthy leader when it reconnects (`withPreVoteARejoiningNodeDoesNotDisruptTheLeader`,
  and its contrast `withoutPreVoteTheSameRejoinDeposesTheLeader`).
- **Check-quorum:** a leader that hasn't heard from a majority for an election timeout steps down.
  Followers that still hear from their leader ignore vote requests (a "lease" on votes).

**Membership: one server at a time (thesis chapter 4).** Adding or removing a single voter keeps
every old majority overlapping every new one, so no joint consensus is needed. The rules:
- A config entry takes effect **when appended**, not when committed. If a conflicting suffix
  removes it, the node falls back to the previous configuration.
- Only **one change at a time**: the previous change must be committed first.
- A new leader must **commit an entry of its own term before changing membership**. This is
  Ongaro's 2015 correction to the single-server algorithm.
- New nodes join as **learners**: they receive the log but don't vote, and are promoted once caught
  up.
- A leader that removes itself keeps leading until the removal commits, then steps down.

**Snapshots.** The host calls `compact(appliedIndex, state)`, which replaces log entries with the
state machine's state. A follower that needs compacted entries gets `InstallSnapshot`. If it
already holds the snapshot's last entry, it only advances its commit index. Otherwise the snapshot
replaces its whole log and state machine.

**ReadIndex (thesis 6.4).** To read without writing to the log, the leader:
1. records its commit index;
2. starts a new heartbeat round;
3. waits until a majority has answered that round.

That proves it was still the leader after the read arrived, so the recorded index covers every
write that completed before the read. The read then runs once the state machine has applied that
far. A leader cut off from its majority can't complete a read. Mutation 4 shows what happens
without this check: a stale read. Leases, which skip the heartbeat round by trusting clocks, are
deliberately not built yet.

## The simulator

`RaftSimulation` runs a whole group in one thread from one seed:
- each node is a real `RaftNode`;
- each stores through `RaftStore` into a real `LsmEngine` on its own `SimFileSystem`;
- they talk over `SimNetwork`, an implementation of the real `Transport` seam.

Faults, all drawn from the seed:
- **Network:** 2% of messages lost, 1% duplicated, 3% delayed by up to 200 ms (so reordered);
  random partitions and isolated nodes.
- **Processes:** power cuts, where unsynced disk data is lost or torn and the process restarts
  after a delay.
- **Disks:** a disk fails partway through a write, and the process dies (crash-only).
- **Membership:** members added as learners, promoted, and removed, including the leader. Removed
  nodes keep running with their stale logs, like real zombies.
- **Knobs** drawn per seed ("buggify"): 3 or 5 voters, 1 to 64 entries per message, 1 to 32 in
  flight, gentle or harsh fault rates.

`RaftChecker` checks continuously:
1. **Election safety:** at most one leader per term.
2. **Log matching:** across every pair of live logs, every half second.
3. **Leader completeness:** every committed entry is in each new leader's log.
4. **State machine safety:** every replica's `HashChain` has the same hash at each index, so they
   applied the same entries in the same order.
5. **Linearizable reads:** each ReadIndex read reflects everything committed before it started.

After the fault period the network heals and every node restarts. The group must then elect a
leader and bring every member past a newly committed entry (liveness). The same seed replays the
identical run (`aSeedReplaysExactly`).

## Verification (the Phase 4 gate)

- **Simulation gate:** 5,000 varied seeds pass in about 4 minutes
  (`-Ddistdb.sim.raftSeeds=5000`); CI runs 100 per build. Over those 5,000 seeds the simulator:
  - committed 1,117,224 entries and elected 28,821 leaders;
  - checked 261,073 linearizable reads;
  - cut power 26,735 times, failed disks mid-write 9,536 times and partitioned the network
    38,281 times;
  - installed 6,725 snapshots and made 20,104 membership changes;
  - lost 3.6 million of 36.4 million messages.

  No safety property broke, and every seed recovered.
- **Unit tests** (`RaftNodeTest`), one per rule, including the paper's tricky cases:
  - Figure 8;
  - a stale follower suffix;
  - a candidate missing committed entries;
  - fast backtracking;
  - a vote remembered across a restart;
  - snapshot catch-up;
  - learner → voter → leader removal;
  - ReadIndex with and without a majority.
- **Codec and storage:** `RaftCodecTest` round-trips 5,000 random messages and rejects truncated
  ones. `RaftStoreTest` checks random Readies against a model, across power cuts.
- **Mutation checks:** each bug was put in on purpose to prove the tests can catch it.

| # | Bug put in on purpose | Caught by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | no election restriction | simulation, seed 1 (leader completeness) |
| 2 | commit earlier-term entries by counting (Figure 8) | unit test only; 500 simulation seeds missed it |
| 3 | follower commits past its last verified entry | unit test only; 500 simulation seeds missed it |
| 4 | ReadIndex without confirming leadership | simulation, seed 9 (stale read) |
| 5 | host sends messages before persisting | simulation, seed 45 (leader completeness) |
| 7 | term and vote not persisted | simulation, seed 5 (two leaders in term 1) |
| 11 | votes for several candidates in a term | simulation, seed 1 (two leaders in term 1) |

Rows 2 and 3 are the lesson of this phase. Random faults don't reach every deep scenario. Figure 8
needs three elections with crashes at exact moments, and this implementation's conflict hints and
full-suffix truncation make the stale-suffix case rare. Those rules need targeted tests, and they
have them.

## Not yet (deliberately)

- Leadership transfer (for the Phase 6 rebalancer).
- Leader leases (reads without a heartbeat round; they need bounded clock drift).
- Batching many groups' messages per connection (Phase 6).
- Writing the log in parallel with sending.
- Chunked snapshots.
- A gRPC `Transport` (Phase 5).
